In Chapter 4 Part 1, Norman focuses on 4 kinds of constraints: Physical, Cultural, Semantic, and Logical. First physical, which is a constraint that tells the user what actions can be done based on the physical characteristics of an item. Cultural constraints are actions seen as appropriate or inappropriate based on social norms. Semantic constraints rely on our knowledge of the situation. Finally, Logical constraints, which has to do with the relationship between the user and how to use an item. When reading this part of the chapter, I find it difficult to differentiate between semantic and cultural constraints, as I feel they overlap the most with one another as opposed to physical and logical constraints. Later in this section, Norman goes into affordances, signifiers, and constraints to everyday objects, where he discusses personal experiences related to everyday objects that have caused problems for the user. Something that comes to mind regarding this topic is when I first started going to the gym.  Many machines do not provide the user with information on how to use certain machines, which can be easily dangerous if the user has not already done their research on gym equipment. This was an issue that prevented me from attempting the gym, as I was reluctant to learn by myself; Luckily, I was able to go with a friend who is already familiar with the environment, so it wasn't as difficult to get accustomed to the equipment as opposed to just watching videos.
