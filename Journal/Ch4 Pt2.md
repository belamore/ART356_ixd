In chapter 4 part 2, Norman explores a number of constraints that prevent a user from committing a specific action. 
These constraints consist of forcing functions, interlocks, lock-ins, lockouts, and also cultural conventions as a constraint. I found the discussion about cultural constraints to be the most interesting because it's a unique restraint that makes it hard for people to get accustomed to; a problem most prevalent in situations where people visit a foreign country, for example, the faucet situation Norman faced when he was in England and had trouble figuring out the mapping. What's also interesting to think about when it comes to this topic is how this constraint makes it difficult for any changes to be made in the systems that we are used to, as humans find it bothersome to learn and adapt to change, even if those changes would be more efficient for us in the long run. This is brought up by Norman when he argues that the metric system is superior to the English scale of units in practically every way, yet the United States is one of the few countries that has chosen to remain with this system, probably for reasons likely related to laziness.
